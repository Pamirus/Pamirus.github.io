Standard Operating Procedures

The text of the in-game book, word for word (about 893 words). Copies lie on the Law Office's shelf, in the Security Office and on the Bridge: hold one and tap Read. See also Station Law, which wins where the two differ.

Purpose

These procedures tell Security how to work and tell everyone else what to expect. They sit under Station Law: where the two differ, Station Law wins. Security protects the crew first, the station second and itself last.

Security duties

Walk the corridors and tubes on a regular round and be seen: a visible officer prevents more trouble than a hidden one solves. Answer calls for help at once. The nearest officer goes and says so on the radio, so that nobody is sent twice and nobody is missed.

Keep the brig clean, lit and supplied with air, keep the cell doors working and look in on whoever is inside.

Take complaints at the Security desk by the public access area, and at the Security Office. Write each one down, and never turn away someone with a problem, even if it is not Security's.

Help the other departments: stand by a doctor treating someone dangerous, escort valuable cargo, and open a door for a crew member who has lost an ID card once you have checked who they are.

Alert levels

The station is always on one of three levels. The Captain sets the level. The Head of Security may raise it from green to blue if the Captain cannot be reached. Only the Captain lowers it.

Green: normal duty. Officers patrol singly, doors work as usual, and a search needs a clear reason and the person's agreement or the Captain's order.

Blue: a danger is suspected but not confirmed, such as a threat, a stolen restricted item or a hull alarm with no cause found. Officers patrol in pairs with their equipment in view and may ask anyone in a corridor to show an ID card. Department heads tell their crews. Every search is recorded with its reason.

Red: a danger is confirmed and present, such as a breach, a power failure in a department, a hostage or a violent death. All officers report to the Security Office and are assigned by the Head of Security, and the brig is made ready. Officers may search anyone in the danger area without asking, though every rule about treating people properly stays. Crew not needed there keep clear.

When the danger has passed, the Captain lowers the level one step at a time, with a word on the radio at each step.

Searching and detaining

Before you search or stop anyone, know your reason and say it in one sentence. If you cannot, do not do it. Ask first, speak plainly and keep your distance. Use the least force that works and stop the moment it is no longer needed.

Search in the open, with a second officer or a witness near. Take only what the rule allows, write down what you took and give it back on release, unless it is evidence of a Class 2 or Class 3 offence, when it goes to the Head of Security.

Detain only for an offence in Station Law, and name its class aloud. Escort the person to the brig yourself by the shortest safe way, stopping nowhere but at a doctor. If they are hurt, ill or short of air, call a doctor before anything else.

Use of the cells

At a cell door's panel, an officer or the Captain picks the time from 1 minute, 2 minutes, 3 minutes, 5 minutes, 7 minutes, 10 minutes, 12 minutes or 15 minutes; the door counts it and shows the time left. Match the class: 1 to 3 minutes for a repeated Class 1 offence, 5 or 7 for Class 2, 10 to 15 for Class 3.

Take belongings first: what the prisoner carries, not what they wear, goes into the cell's property locker, which only security opens, and returns on release if they are still in the cell or brig. The cell doors and the way out by the hallway open from inside for anyone, so a released prisoner walks out alone, never into the tunnels.

Lock the door only with the person inside, one person to a cell unless the Head of Security says otherwise, and never someone who needs a doctor. While it is locked nobody opens it from either side, with a card or a tool. An officer or the Captain may release it early at the door, and should if the person falls ill, if a Lawyer's answer says so, or if the cell loses air.

A cell holds people who have been charged. It is not a store, a hiding place or a way of keeping someone past the sentence. Write the time, the sentence, the person's name and your own in the record.

Reporting

Report by radio as soon as it is safe: what is happening, what you are doing, where you are. Keep it short. Afterwards write the full report: who, what, where and when, then what was done and what was found. Leave out guesses, or mark them as guesses.

Hand the report to the Head of Security, or to the Captain if there is none, and a copy of any detention to the Law Office. If you made a mistake, say so in it: a mistake that is reported can be mended.

A complaint against an officer is taken as seriously as any other and goes at once to the Head of Security and the Law Office.
